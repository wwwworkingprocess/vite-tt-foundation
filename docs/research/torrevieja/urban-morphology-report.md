# Torrevieja Urban Morphology Report

## Executive summary

Torrevieja is best represented procedurally as a **system of locally repetitive but strongly contrasting urban fabrics**, not as one city-wide random building field. The core is a compact, fine-grain grid around the civic center and harbor; north and south of it are apartment and mixed residential belts; farther west and northwest are extensive low-rise villa urbanizations; the coast repeatedly shifts toward apartment/slab morphologies; Habaneras/Casagrande/Industrial Salinas form a road-oriented commercial/industrial system; La Mata remains a distinct northern settlement; and La Hoya/La Manguilla are active development-frontier areas. The two salt lagoons, their protected-land context, the Mediterranean edge and major road corridors strongly constrain where continuous urban fabric exists. [SRC-002] [SRC-010] [SRC-016] [SRC-017] [SRC-020]

This V0 therefore uses 13 semantic districts containing 31 morphology zones, with 11 reusable urban profiles and 10 building-family profiles. Most district and zone polygons are deliberately **research approximations**. Their purpose is to tell a generator what grammar is plausible at a location, not to assert legal neighborhood boundaries. The central procedural principle is: **low-to-moderate variation inside a coherent zone; strong variation between zones and districts**. Population remains a separate quantitative field and should modulate each grammar rather than redefine it.

Readiness is **needs-review**. The source-supported urban-form distinctions are strong enough for prototype generation, but boundary geometry must be checked in GIS against current official planning and orthophoto data before it becomes production spatial truth.

## Research method and source hierarchy

Research followed the requested hierarchy. Municipal planning fichas and current Ayuntamiento publications were used first for planning-area names, permitted forms, development status, transport relationships and major anchors. Generalitat/ICV material was used for protected-land and base-cartography context. Copernicus Urban Atlas and OpenStreetMap were used as cross-checks for land-use continuity, street topology and POI location. A municipal aerial-image resource was inspected for morphology inference; no aerial or satellite imagery is reproduced in these files. [SRC-001] [SRC-016] [SRC-018] [SRC-019] [SRC-020] [SRC-028]

Four evidence classes are kept separate throughout: **Source-supported fact** means a statement is directly grounded in a cited source ID; **Visual inference** means a pattern was inferred from aerial/open-map form and is marked with confidence; **Research interpretation** is the semantic grouping or procedural district/zone definition created for this package; and **Procedural recommendation** describes how a future generator should use the evidence. Approximate metric ranges are intentionally rounded and should not be read as cadastral measurements.

The municipal 2024 technical report's ten 'ámbitos territoriales homogéneos' are especially useful as a broad spatial scaffold, but they are reference-value/tax-analysis areas, not official neighborhood boundaries. This package never promotes them to barrio status. [SRC-010]

## Geographic and environmental structure

Torrevieja's urbanized settlement occupies a constrained eastern/southern band between the Mediterranean and the lagoon system. Official Generalitat material describes the Parque Natural de las Lagunas de La Mata y Torrevieja as a two-lagoon protected system; the brochure gives an overall park area of about 3,700 ha, including roughly 1,400 ha of Laguna de Torrevieja and 700 ha of Laguna de La Mata. [SRC-016] [SRC-017]

The lagoon system produces unusually strong non-urban edges: west of the main city and behind the northern coastal settlement, buildable urban fabric stops or thins rather than fading continuously into ordinary suburbia. The Mediterranean creates a second hard edge and is associated with promenade, beach, marina and apartment-strip morphologies. Major roads—especially the N-332/bypass system and routes connecting CV-905/CV-95 corridors—act both as barriers and as access spines for large retail, industrial and institutional parcels. [SRC-020] [SRC-021]

V0 landscape polygons are intentionally coarse. They are sufficient to suppress obviously implausible ordinary-building generation in lagoon/water/protected contexts, but they must not be used for regulatory or environmental decisions. The official protected-area layer should replace the coarse mask in any production GIS pipeline. [SRC-016]

## Urban development pattern at city scale

Municipal planning material characterizes Torrevieja through a compact urban expansion/core, dispersed garden-city-style residential nuclei and economic/industrial nuclei. That high-level description matches the morphology found in the planning fichas: large low-rise plots in Los Balcones/La Torreta/El Chaparral/San Luis; mixed residential typologies in La Veleta/Molino Blanco/Nueva Torrevieja; and explicit industrial areas at Casagrande and Industrial Salinas. [SRC-002] [SRC-003] [SRC-004] [SRC-005] [SRC-006] [SRC-007] [SRC-008] [SRC-009] [SRC-014] [SRC-015]

At city scale the generator should therefore switch grammars rather than continuously randomize parameters. The most important discontinuities are: compact center → mixed apartment belts; mixed belts → villa/compound urbanizations; residential → big-box/industrial superblocks; inland residential → coastal apartment/slab strips; and urban fabric → protected lagoon/open-land edges. La Hoya and La Manguilla require a temporal distinction as well, because current municipal publications show ongoing urbanization/housing activity rather than a stable finished fabric. [SRC-022] [SRC-023] [SRC-024]

## Street and block morphology

The V0 profiles intentionally separate street grammar from building-family selection. The central grid uses the highest regularity and shortest blocks; La Mata uses a compact village-scale variant; inner apartment districts use semi-orthogonal blocks; planned villa/compound districts use longer blocks and more locally adaptive geometry; coastal apartment strips use larger plots and a coast-responsive axis; commercial/industrial/institutional zones use arterial superblocks or industrial access. This hierarchy should prevent the current procedural renderer from applying oversized residential objects everywhere.

Orientation values in the JSON are dominant local axes only. They are useful for deterministic procedural alignment, but they should be replaced or overridden by real street bearings once road GeoJSON becomes authoritative. At V0, a zone's regularity and orientation behavior matter more than exact bearing.

## Parcel and building morphology

Parcel scale is one of the clearest differentiators. The center uses fine-grain frontages and little setback; apartment belts move to medium-grain parcels; villa urbanizations use detached lots with strong front/side setbacks; terrace/compound developments repeat modules around communal open space; big-box and industrial districts use large plots and service/parking yards. Planning fichas provide particularly strong scale controls for Los Balcones, La Torreta, El Chaparral, San Luis and Industrial Salinas. [SRC-003] [SRC-004] [SRC-005] [SRC-006] [SRC-015]

Building profiles are families, not replicas. The generator should use a compact Mediterranean palette—white/cream/sand render, flat/terrace roofs in apartment fabric, terracotta/hip/gable or flat/terrace combinations in villas, and simple industrial roofs for sheds. The metadata deliberately avoids exotic architecture or facade-specific claims not supported by the research.

## Local repetition versus city-wide variation

Torrevieja's procedural identity should come from **repeated local development languages**. Villa streets can repeat plot widths, two-storey envelopes and roof families; terrace compounds can repeat rows and communal courts; apartment developments can repeat balcony/slab rhythms; industrial blocks can repeat sheds and yards. The city-wide variety comes from moving between those coherent systems. Random per-building variation would make the result less believable, not more.

Accordingly, `repetitionBehavior` is encoded in every building profile and each zone uses a weighted family mix. The mix should be sampled at block/development scale where possible, not independently for every parcel.

## Density and population-field interpretation

The existing population field remains the quantitative authority. Settlement metadata is an **overlay** that answers what a given density should look like. A high population cell inside the central grid can increase infill probability, select the higher part of the 3–6-storey typical range or strengthen mixed-use frontage. The same numeric density inside a villa zone should not suddenly produce center blocks; it can instead raise occupied-lot share, choose semi-detached forms more often or use the denser end of the permitted low-rise family.

Likewise, population values should not override categorical land-use constraints. Industrial, commercial, institutional, water and protected-land zones can have nonzero surrounding population without becoming residential. La Hoya/La Manguilla need an additional temporal rule: current population should not be mistaken for fully built future morphology.

## Commercial / industrial morphology

Habaneras is treated as arterial big-box commercial fabric: large boxes, parking/service courts and access-oriented superblocks. Casagrande and Industrial Salinas are treated as industrial-access fabrics with sheds/warehouses, yards and low storey counts. Municipal planning sources directly support the industrial status and low-rise/large-parcel logic of Casagrande and Industrial Salinas; the Habaneras municipal page and transport network confirm its activity-anchor role. ## Coastal and tourism-related morphology

Coastal Torrevieja is not a single morphology. The center/harbor has compact mixed urban frontage; Los Locos is an urban beach/apartment corridor; Cabo Cervera/Torre del Moro and parts of the southern coast use larger-plot slab/tower forms; La Mata combines a compact settlement core with a beachfront apartment edge; Molino del Agua provides a meaningful open-space break. ## Urban fringe and settlement edges

The strongest edge types are lagoon/protected-land, Mediterranean water, major-road infrastructure and active-development frontiers. Villa urbanizations often taper or fragment toward protected/open land; the center has a hard urban/water edge; industrial/commercial zones meet roads abruptly; La Hoya and parts of La Manguilla have construction/vacancy edges rather than mature neighborhood edges.

A procedural city generator should make edges explicit. It should reduce parcel fill near fragmented fringes, reserve larger open buffers where protected-land context begins, and avoid continuing a regular grid through roads/water/wetland masks merely because the population raster has residual values.

## Procedural-generation implications

At the population-field stage, sample district and morphology-zone membership as categorical/semantic context. At the route stage, use zone street regularity/orientation to infer secondary structure around the provisional corridors. At the block stage, use profile block-size ranges and edge behavior. At the parcel stage, use parcel pattern, frontage/depth, coverage and setbacks. At the building stage, draw from the zone's weighted building-family mix with deterministic seeds and block/development-level repetition. Before any ordinary parcel generation, reserve explicit landmarks and landscape masks.

The metadata should influence **selection and constraints**, not prescribe every street or footprint. That separation is what lets V0 survive later road-data improvements.

## Recommended generator hierarchy

Recommended hierarchy: **population field → district lookup → morphology-zone lookup → landscape/landmark exclusions → provisional route/corridor interpretation → local street grammar → blocks → parcels → development-scale building-family selection → deterministic buildings**. Districts provide broad semantic context; zones provide the actual urban grammar; urban profiles provide reusable geometric rules; building profiles provide family-level massing; landmarks and landscape regions reserve exceptions.

## Recommended scale relationships

Use the JSON ranges as order-of-magnitude targets rather than fixed dimensions. Central blocks are roughly tens of metres per side with fine frontages; apartment blocks broaden toward roughly 70–190 m; villa/compound blocks commonly extend toward 80–230 m; commercial/industrial superblocks can reach several hundred metres. Local streets generally occupy the high-single-digits to teens of metres, while major commercial/institutional access can be wider. Villa plots are materially deeper/wider and less covered than center parcels; big-box/industrial parcels are an order of magnitude coarser than fine central frontages.

Renderer scaling should therefore be morphology-dependent. A building asset that is plausible in an industrial superblock can be absurd in the old central grid; a center frontage module should not be stretched to fill a Los Balcones plot. The urban/building profile split exists specifically to enforce that relationship.

## How the metadata should enrich the population field

Conceptually, each population raster cell should retain its original quantitative value and gain references such as `districtId`, `zoneId`, optional nearby `landmarkIds`, and landscape/edge flags. The generator can then interpret the same density differently by morphology. Where a cell straddles polygons, use area-weighted or center-point assignment consistently and record the chosen policy; do not rewrite the source population value to make it fit the urban interpretation.

A future enriched cell can therefore be read as: **population value + district semantic context + zone grammar + corridor context + constraints**. The population field remains authoritative for quantity; this package supplies spatial/semantic meaning.

## What this metadata must NOT become authoritative for

This V0 must not become authoritative for cadastral parcels, property boundaries, zoning entitlements, legal protected-area limits, exact road centerlines, exact building heights, demographic composition, employment, tourism demand, land values, fares, objectives or game-balance multipliers. Planning-derived parameters are used as morphology evidence, not as a substitute for live planning law. Research polygons are not official neighborhood boundaries.

## Future GeoJSON / road-network migration path

**V0:** provisional route polylines plus morphology-zone street grammar. **V1:** more accurate route GeoJSON plus the same zone grammars. **V2:** real road/street GeoJSON replaces generated primary/secondary street geometry, while district/zone metadata continues to control parcel subdivision, setbacks, open-space behavior and building-family selection. Because orientation and street regularity are separated from the building profiles, real streets can override geometry without deleting the morphology semantics.

District and zone polygons themselves should also become versioned GeoJSON features later. Stable IDs in this package should be preserved where semantics remain valid, even if boundaries are redrawn.

## Data quality and uncertainty

Strongest evidence is plan-level: low-rise villa rules in Los Balcones/La Torreta/El Chaparral/San Luis, mixed typologies in La Veleta/Molino Blanco/Nueva Torrevieja, industrial status in Casagrande/Industrial Salinas and official protected-land context. Weakest evidence is the exact placement of V0 morphology transitions. Those boundaries were intentionally kept coarse and conservative.

**Validation results for this delivery:** all 13 district IDs referenced by zones exist; all 31 zone IDs referenced by districts/landmarks exist; all urban-profile and building-profile references resolve; all 35 source IDs resolve and every registered source is used; all 31 building-profile mixes sum to 1.0; all polygon rings are closed and pass a simple-polygon validity check; all coordinates are stored as longitude, latitude in EPSG:4326 and fall inside the declared research bounds; all assigned landmark points fall within their coarse district/zone envelope; and no research-defined district is labeled official. These checks validate internal consistency, not geographic authority.

Current-change risk is highest in La Hoya, La Manguilla, Los Locos public realm and Eras de la Sal. [SRC-022] [SRC-023] [SRC-024] [SRC-025] [SRC-026]

## Recommended manual review before project integration

Perform a GIS overlay against current ICV/PNOA or equivalent orthophoto and downloadable municipal planning geometry; redraw district/zone boundaries where obvious streets, roads or protected-land limits provide better separators; inspect overlaps/gaps and decide whether the application's metadata lookup requires a strict non-overlapping partition; sample real blocks/parcels in every reusable profile to calibrate the approximate ranges; verify current build-out in La Hoya and La Manguilla; and replace coarse protected-land/water masks with official geometries before any production constraint logic.

## Research conclusion

Torrevieja can be represented compactly without reproducing every street or building. Its useful procedural grammar is the contrast among a dense central grid, several distinct apartment/coastal fabrics, highly repetitive low-rise villa and terrace urbanizations, large commercial/industrial superblocks, institutional campuses, a separate La Mata settlement, active development frontiers and unusually strong lagoon/coastal constraints. The V0 metadata captures those differences with reusable profiles and explicit uncertainty. It is suitable as **sample production input after manual spatial review**, and it should remain an enrichment layer over population rather than becoming a substitute for authoritative GIS, planning or demographic data.
